      History and the four-year cycle

      In US dollars, the usual measure for cycles

      Every four years or so, the number of new bitcoins created is cut in half (a "halving"). In every cycle so far, a big rise followed each halving, then a deep fall. Here is how the pattern looks.

      The long view

       
      Price 200-week average Bitcoin halving

      Log scale: each step up the side is the same percentage rise, so 2013 and 2025 can share one chart. The 200-week average has marked the floor of most past crashes.

      Cycles side by side

      Each line starts on a halving day at 1× (the price that day). It shows how many times higher or lower Bitcoin was in the days that followed. Each cycle has risen less than the one before.

      Year by year ·

      Timeline since 2008

        A cycle's peak here means its highest price in the 2½ years after the halving. Only four cycles have ever happened, which is a small sample. Past patterns are useful context, not a forecast.
